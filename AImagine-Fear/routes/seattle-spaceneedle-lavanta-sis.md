The weather is a cool night with the marine fog layer pushing in off Puget Sound. Her two bare legs and bare feet fill the bottom third of the frame, wet and shining on clear glass, and straight through the glass between her own feet the structure of the tower drops away beneath her before she has moved at all. The floor is turning, slowly and steadily, so the whole world outside is sliding past her while she stands still. Her toes are splayed on clear glass with the tower legs visible directly beneath them. The transparent slide mouth waits ahead, its pale lavender rims the only saturated colour in the frame, and it drifts steadily toward her without her taking a step. Below her feet there is no ground, only the flat top of the fog with the downtown towers standing out of it. She does not move yet. The stillness is the fear, and the floor is moving her toward it anyway.

[2.0-4.0] She edges forward toward the mouth in short unsteady steps, and the rotation adds to every one of them so she arrives faster than she means to. Behind it the fog keeps its exact place in the frame and the slide is visible entering it far below, a lavender line going into grey. Her legs shake visibly.

[4.0-6.0] She sits and drops into the throat of the slide. The deck leaves the frame entirely and does not come back, and with it the sideways drift stops dead for the first time. The first steep pitch takes her straight at the fog and the top of the fog rises to meet her feet.

[6.0-7.8] She goes into the fog. Grey closes around the slide on both sides and the towers are gone. Water beads crawl sideways across the lens. Her legs are the only hard edges in the frame.

[7.8-10.0] She comes out of the bottom of the fog and Seattle is suddenly close. [10.0-12.5] The slide banks hard into one long continuous curve carried on thin struts, already visible ahead before she reaches it. [12.5-15.0] Straight ahead and growing, a long lavender-lit pool on a pier rooftop over Elliott Bay waits with its far edge open to the water. The final chute aims at it. She rises and breaks through at the open edge, where the water runs over the lip and falls away into the dark.
