## BEATS

[0.0-2.0] From the very first moment the rider is already walking briskly barefoot along the narrow glass walkway toward the slide, left foot, right foot, never stopping. Through the clear glass under her feet there is only empty air and the land far below. The open mouth of the slide grows with every step, its two arctic blue rims curving away and down toward the cloud, and Mount Fuji waits far below and ahead beyond it. She never stands still and never hesitates.

[2.0-4.0] She reaches the mouth, sits straight down into it feet first and pushes off, and the slide takes her at once into the steep plunge. Mount Fuji fills the centre of the frame, its snow-capped summit and the wide jagged rim of the crater sharp against the blue sky, long streaks of snow running down the dark rock. The slide drops past the crater rim close enough to see the loose black cinders, the frozen snow crust and the red-brown lava rock rush up and past on the left, the autumn forests far below. Below, the white sea of cloud rushes up toward her and the slide dives straight into it.

[4.0-6.0] The slide plunges into the cloud. The frame becomes an even, flat grey-white murk; only her two legs, both feet and the two arctic blue rims stay readable. The air turns hot and smells of sulphur, the murk inside the cloud darkens to dirty grey, and a deep rumble shakes the acrylic as fine ash settles on the lens.

[6.0-8.5] She drops out of the underside of the cloud into the same mountain erupting. A towering black column of ash blasts out of the summit crater of Mount Fuji into a dark brown sky, lit from below by fountains of orange lava, and the snow on the upper slopes is bursting into white steam. Lightning crackles inside the ash cloud. Glowing lava bombs arc out of the crater and smash into the autumn forests, setting them on fire.

[8.5-10.5] The column grows taller and starts to sag. Ash rains down thickly, grey flakes streaking across the frame, and red-hot stones thud onto the slide, bouncing off the rims and leaving glowing scorch marks on the acrylic. A lava bomb bursts on the slope right beside the slide in a shower of sparks, and a river of orange lava pours down through the snow, throwing up white steam where it meets the ice.

[10.5-13.0] Part of the ash column falls back onto the mountain and becomes a pyroclastic flow, a boiling avalanche of grey-black ash, steam and hot gas pouring down the snowy cone of Mount Fuji toward the slide from the right side of the frame, glowing red at its base, with burning pines and black rocks tumbling inside it, growing every second and filling more and more of the frame.

[13.0-15.0] The slide runs straight into it, both legs and both feet still separately visible and the two arctic blue rims still burning. The pyroclastic flow rolls over the camera: hot ash blasts across the lens, the frame floods glowing red-orange, then dims to near black as thick grey ash settles over the lens and buries it.
