## OPENING STATE

The rider stands barefoot on the summit walkway at the top of the steel arch of the Sydney Harbour Bridge in Sydney, at night. The weather is a cool night with a low sea cloud drifting across the harbour. Her two bare legs and bare feet fill the bottom third of the frame, wet and shining. The riveted steel of the arch runs away under her feet on both sides. Ahead and below, a low sheet of sea cloud drifts across the harbour and the white shells of the Sydney Opera House stand lit just beyond the near edge of it. Far ahead on the walkway, small in the distance, sits the open mouth of a fully transparent acrylic slide, its two upswept rims outlined by a continuous jade green strip. There is no railing anywhere between her feet and the drop.

## BEATS

[0.0-2.0] The rider stands still on the crown of the arch and the camera on her chest holds the drop dead centre. Her toes flex against the cold riveted steel. The transparent slide mouth waits ahead of her, its jade green rims the only saturated colour in the frame. Below and ahead, the low sheet of sea cloud slides across the harbour and the lit white shells of the Opera House sit at the far edge of it. She does not move yet. The stillness is the fear.

[2.0-4.0] She edges forward toward the mouth in short unsteady steps. The mouth grows continuously and never jumps in size: first a small outline, then a pair of upswept transparent rims taller than her shins, then a wide open throat. Behind it the harbour keeps its exact place in the frame and the slide is visible running off the arch and down into the drifting cloud. Her legs shake visibly.

[4.0-6.0] She sits and drops into the throat of the slide. The arch leaves the frame entirely and does not come back. The two jade green rims snap into hard converging perspective, running away from her hips out over the water, and the solid clear floor between them is readable the whole way down. The first steep pitch takes her and the lit shells of the Opera House swing up the side of the frame as she falls past their height.

[6.0-7.8] She goes into the sea cloud. Pale grey closes around the slide and the harbour is gone. The clear floor under her legs stays transparent and the light on the water keeps reading through it, dimmer but never absent, a wide silver sheet moving under her feet. The two jade green rims burn at exactly the same brightness they had in open air, converging ahead into grey with no end visible. Cold beads crawl sideways across the lens. Her legs are the only hard edges in the frame.

[7.8-10.0] She comes out under the cloud and the harbour is right at her feet. The black water runs directly beneath the clear floor, close enough to read the chop on it, and the lit white shells of the Opera House now stand up beside her instead of below. The slide bottoms out and a wall of white water bursts up around her hips and across the lens, lit jade green from beneath by the rims, then clears in streaks. The rims are streaming past the frame edges.

[10.0-12.5] The slide banks hard into one long continuous curve carried on thin struts, already visible ahead before she reaches it. The whole harbour rotates around her legs while her legs stay in the same place in the frame. The lit shells tilt up one side of the frame and the dark water fills the other. Spray flies outward along the wall of the turn. Coming out of it the horizon settles far lower, and the steel arch she started from is a black line above her, its crown lost in the cloud.

[12.5-15.0] Straight ahead and growing, a long green-lit swimming pool set into a stone harbour wall waits with the open water on the other side of its far edge. The final chute aims at it. She hits the water, the frame goes to churning white for a beat, then punches through into the pool: bright, bubble-filled, muffled under the surface. She rises and breaks through, and the shells of the Opera House are standing lit and close beside her.

## END STATE

The rider holds the tiled edge of a long green-lit swimming pool set into a stone harbour wall in Sydney, shoulders out of the water, looking up. Her two bare feet trail behind her and break the surface in the lower third of the frame. Water still churns from her entry. The bowed fisheye horizon holds the lit white shells of the Sydney Opera House close beside the pool, the dark harbour past the far wall, and the black steel arch of the bridge going up into the sea cloud behind them.
