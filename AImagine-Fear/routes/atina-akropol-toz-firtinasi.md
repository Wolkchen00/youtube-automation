## BEATS

[0.0-2.0] From the very first moment the rider is already walking briskly barefoot along the narrow glass walkway toward the slide, left foot, right foot, never stopping. Through the clear glass under her feet there is only empty air and the land far below. The open mouth of the slide grows with every step, its two electric blue rims curving away and down toward the cloud, and the Acropolis waits far below and ahead beyond it. She never stands still and never hesitates.

[2.0-4.0] She reaches the mouth, sits straight down into it feet first and pushes off, and the slide takes her at once into the steep plunge. The Acropolis fills the centre of the frame, the Parthenon standing on the summit of its rock, every fluted marble column and the broken stone gable sharp in the morning sun. The slide drops past the corner of the Parthenon close enough to see the cracked honey-coloured marble blocks and the grooves of the columns rush up and past on the left, the ancient stone stairs and the olive trees on the slopes far below. Below, the white sea of cloud rushes up toward her and the slide dives straight into it.

[4.0-6.0] The slide plunges into the cloud. The frame becomes an even, flat grey-white murk; only her two legs, both feet and the two electric blue rims stay readable. The air turns hot and dry, the murk inside the cloud turns a dusty orange, and the first fine grains of grit start to hiss against the lens.

[6.0-8.5] She drops out of the underside of the cloud into the same city swallowed by a Saharan dust storm. The Parthenon stands pale on its rock under a deep orange sky, and beyond it a towering wall of orange dust taller than the Acropolis rolls in from the sea across the whole horizon, burying the white city street by street. Lightning flickers inside the wall. Dust streams sideways across the lens.

[8.5-10.5] Lightning cracks out of the dust wall and strikes the hillside below the Acropolis, lighting every marble column white for an instant, and thunder shakes the slide. Gusts throw grit across the frame in long orange streaks, the slide shudders and bucks, and dust hisses over the clear floor under her feet. Lycabettus is already gone inside the orange, and the shadow of the storm races across the rooftops toward the rock.

[10.5-13.0] The dust wall reaches the foot of the Acropolis and climbs the cliffs, swallowing the olive trees and the old stone walls. It rolls straight toward the slide from the right side of the frame, a boiling cliff of dark orange dust with torn branches, roof tiles and flying grit inside it, lit by flickers of lightning, growing every second and filling more and more of the frame.

[13.0-15.0] The slide runs straight into it, both legs and both feet still separately visible and the two electric blue rims still burning. The dust wall slams into the camera: grit blasts across the lens, the frame goes solid burning orange, then dims to near black as dust piles over the lens.
