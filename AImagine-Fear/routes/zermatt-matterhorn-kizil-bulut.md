## OPENING STATE

The rider stands barefoot on the observation deck at Glacier Paradise, facing the Matterhorn above Zermatt, at night. The weather is a freezing night with the banner cloud streaming sideways off the ridge. Her two bare legs and bare feet fill the bottom third of the frame, wet and shining on frozen steel plate, and her breath shows in the frame in short bursts. Ahead of her the Matterhorn stands as a single black wedge against the stars, and off its ridge a long cloud streams away sideways, fast and flat, like smoke off a chimney in wind. It does not lie below her. It crosses in front of her. Beyond the deck edge there is no ground at all, only the dark glacier far down. Far ahead along the deck, small in the distance, sits the open mouth of a fully transparent acrylic slide, its two upswept rims outlined by a continuous crimson strip. It runs off the deck edge and down through the streaming cloud. There is no railing anywhere between her feet and the drop.

## BEATS

[0.0-2.0] The rider stands still at the deck edge and the camera on her chest holds the drop dead centre. Her bare soles are stuck to freezing steel and every toe is rigid. The transparent slide mouth waits ahead of her, its crimson rims the only saturated colour in the frame. Below her feet there is no ground, only black air and the pale glacier a long way down. Across the frame ahead, the banner cloud tears sideways off the Matterhorn ridge without stopping. She does not move yet. The stillness is the fear.

[2.0-4.0] She edges forward toward the mouth in short unsteady steps, and her feet stick slightly to the frozen plate each time. The mouth grows continuously and never jumps in size: first a small outline, then a pair of upswept transparent rims taller than her shins, then a wide open throat. Behind it the banner keeps streaming across at the same speed and the slide is visible entering it, a crimson line going into moving white. Her legs shake visibly.

[4.0-6.0] She sits and drops into the throat of the slide. The deck leaves the frame entirely and does not come back. The two crimson rims snap into hard converging perspective, running away from her hips toward the glacier below, and the solid clear floor between them is readable the whole way down. The first steep pitch takes her straight at the streaming cloud, which is crossing her path instead of waiting under it.

[6.0-7.8] She goes into the banner and it is moving sideways at speed. White closes around the slide on both sides and the Matterhorn is gone, but this white is not still: it rips across the frame left to right, so the slide feels like it is being dragged sideways even though the rims run dead straight ahead. The clear floor under her legs stays transparent and the lights of Zermatt keep reading through it far below, dimmer but never absent, a small warm cluster sliding under her feet. The two crimson rims burn at exactly the same brightness they had in open air, converging ahead into moving white with no end visible. Ice crystals streak the lens instead of water beads. Her legs are the only hard edges in the frame.

[7.8-10.0] She comes out the far side of the banner and the glacier is suddenly close. Blue-white ice runs directly beneath the clear floor, split by black crevasses that pass under her one after another, with the valley opening ahead and the lights of Zermatt at the bottom of it. The slide bottoms out and a wall of white water bursts up around her hips and across the lens, lit crimson from beneath by the rims, then clears in streaks and instantly starts to freeze at the edges. Speed becomes obvious: the rims are streaming past the frame edges.

[10.0-12.5] The slide banks hard into one long continuous curve carried on thin struts, already visible ahead before she reaches it. The whole valley rotates around her legs while her legs stay in the same place in the frame. The black rock wall tilts up one side and the pale glacier fills the other. Spray flies outward along the wall of the turn and hangs in the cold air behind her. Coming out of it the horizon settles far lower and the Matterhorn is behind her now, its banner still tearing off the ridge above.

[12.5-15.0] Straight ahead and growing, a long crimson-lit thermal pool on a terrace above Zermatt waits with its far edge open to the valley, steam standing off its surface in the freezing air. The final chute aims at it. She hits the water, the frame goes to churning white for a beat, then punches through into the pool: warm, bright, bubble-filled, muffled under the surface. She rises and breaks through at the open edge, where the water runs over the lip and falls away into the dark.
